Strain Window

Kernel Warping cut the E11​ standard deviation of the 2862-point grid to 1161 microstrain. VIC-2D's own measurement of the same stretch has a standard deviation of 1385 microstrain. That page also ran an exploratory test. A 15x15-point least-squares window cut the 1161 to 39 microstrain.

This page derives that window. Each strain comes from a fit to a neighborhood of tracked points. The fit averages the tracking error over the neighborhood before it takes a derivative. The price is spatial resolution. The derivation puts a number on both sides of that trade.

Local Displacement Model

The tracked points sit on a regular reference grid with spacing h. Point i has reference position Xi​ and tracked current position xi​. Its displacement is ui​=xi​−Xi​.

A window holds n×n points, with n=2m+1. It centers on point c. Its extent is nh. For n=15 and h=5 px, the extent is 75 px. The distance between the two outermost points along one axis is (n−1)h=70 px. Every "window spanning" figure in this book uses the extent nh.

Point k in the window has local coordinates

sk​=Xk​−Xc​=h(jk​,lk​),jk​,lk​∈{−m,…,m}.

Each displacement component α∈{x,y} gets an affine fit:

uα​(s)≈aα​+Hαx​sx​+Hαy​sy​.

The coefficients Hαβ​ form the displacement gradient H=∂u/∂X at the window center. The fit uses reference coordinates, so H is the Lagrangian gradient. The deformation gradient follows directly:

F=I+H.

Fitting the current positions xα​ instead gives F as the slope. Both routes agree, because X is exactly affine in s.

Least-Squares Estimator

Stack the n2 window points. The design matrix A∈Rn2×3 has one row (1,sx,k​,sy,k​) per point. The vector uα​ holds component α of the n2 displacements. The weights wk​ fill the diagonal matrix W. The fit minimizes

k∑​wk​(uα,k​−Ak​θα​)2,θα​=​aα​Hαx​Hαy​​​.

The normal equations give the solution:

θα​=(A⊤WA)−1A⊤Wuα​.

The grid is regular, so the matrix (A⊤WA)−1A⊤W depends only on n, h, and W. It does not depend on the images.

Uniform Weights

This page sets wk​=1 for every point. Uniform weights give a closed-form answer and the lowest variance for independent errors. A tent or Gaussian weight would reduce the influence of the boundary points of each window. Both are candidate refinements for a later stage. Neither appears here.

A full window is symmetric about its center. So ∑k​sx,k​=∑k​sy,k​=∑k​sx,k​sy,k​=0, and A⊤A becomes diagonal. Each gradient component reduces to one sum:

Hαx​=∑k​sx,k2​∑k​sx,k​uα,k​​,k∑​sx,k2​=h2n3m(m+1)(2m+1)​=3h2n2m(m+1)​.

The y component follows by symmetry. This estimator is a derivative kernel along one axis and a box average along the other.

Strain

The strain measures reuse F from the previous section. Green-Lagrange strain is

E=21​(F⊤F−I).

Log strain, also called Hencky strain, is

Elog​=lnU,U=F⊤F​.

This page reports log strain, to match the figures on the pages before it.

Each window yields one strain tensor, at the window center. A Q4 element from Finite Element Method yields four, one per Gauss point.

A Q4 element is the zero-redundancy case of this model. It fits 4 nodal displacements with 4 bilinear coefficients. It interpolates and averages nothing. A window fits n2 displacements with 3 coefficients. For n=15, that is 225 displacements per 3 coefficients. The redundancy does the averaging.

Noise Gain

Suppose the tracked displacements carry independent errors with variance σ2. The estimator's variance follows from the diagonal A⊤A:

Var(Hαx​)=∑k​sx,k2​σ2​=h2n2m(m+1)3σ2​.

The standard deviation is

std(Hαx​)=hσ​⋅n1​m(m+1)3​​≈hn212​σ​.

The baseline is a Q4 element with side h, evaluated at a Gauss point (η=±1/3​). Its gradient is h1​[a(u2​−u1​)+b(u3​−u4​)] with a+b=1 and a2+b2=32​. So its standard deviation is 4/3​σ/h=1.155σ/h.

Independent errors would cut Kernel Warping's 1161 microstrain to about 16 at n=15. The exploratory test measured 39. The gap has one likely cause. The tracking error is a spatially correlated wave, and a window averages a wave less completely than it averages independent noise. The next section quantifies how much of the wave survives.

Bias and Resolution

A window reduces variance and adds bias. Two results size the bias.

Cubic term. Let the true field be u=6c​s3, with c=∂3u/∂X3. The true gradient at the center is 0. The fitted slope is

Hfit​=6c​∑s2∑s4​=30ch2(3m2+3m−1)​.

The standard deviation from the previous section falls as n−2. This bias grows as n2. The mean squared error, variance plus squared bias, therefore has a minimum. Its window size depends on the field's third derivative and on σ.

A quadratic fit gives the same center slope as the affine fit. A quadratic term is even about the center, and the slope is odd. In a symmetric window, the two are orthogonal. Only a cubic fit removes the bias above, and it raises the variance.

Frequency response. Take u=Asin(kX) and a window half-width a=mh. In the continuum limit, the ratio of the fitted slope to the true slope at the window center is

T(k)=(ka)33(sin(ka)−kacos(ka))​≈1−10(ka)2​for ka≪1.

The small-ka limit matches the cubic bias above.

Kernel Warping found a bias wave in locate_subpixel with a 51 px period. Its wavenumber is k=2π/51. For n=15 and h=5 px, the discrete sum gives T=−0.017. The window keeps 1.7% of that wave's slope, and reverses its sign. The window's 75 px extent spans 1.5 periods of that wave. A fractional number of periods leaves a residue in the fit, and T sizes that residue.

Window Edges

Near the grid boundary, the window truncates. Every tracked point keeps a strain. The affine fit uses the general normal equations, because A⊤A is no longer diagonal.

Truncation costs precision in two ways. Take the x gradient of a window that lost its −x half, with m+1 columns left.

Variance. The slope variance is σ2 divided by ∑k​(sx,k​−sˉx​)2. That sum falls from h2nm(m+1)(2m+1)/3 to h2nm(m+1)(m+2)/12. The standard deviation rises by the factor

m+24(2m+1)​​.

At m=7, the factor is 2.58. At a corner, the window loses half its rows too. The factor becomes

(m+1)(m+2)4(2m+1)2​​,

which is 3.54 at m=7.

In units of σ/h, the standard deviation is 0.0154 in the interior, 0.0398 on an edge, and 0.0545 at a corner. The Q4 element gives 1.155. The edge value is 29 times lower than that. The corner value is 21 times lower.

Location. For a field that is quadratic, u=2κ​s2, an affine fit returns the true gradient at the window's centroid, not at its center. This holds for any point set symmetric about its own centroid, and a truncated window is one. Its centroid sits mh/2 from the window's center. At m=7 and h=5 px, that shift is 17.5 px. The strain error at an edge point is κ⋅mh/2, with κ=∂2u/∂X2.

Uniform stretch has κ=0, so this bias vanishes there. A field with curvature near an edge does show it.

Overlapping Windows

A window can step by 1 point, so every tracked point gets a strain. Or it can step by n points, so no two windows share a point.

At a step of 1, neighboring windows share n2−n of their n2 points. For n=15, that is 210 of 225. Their errors correlate. The standard deviation across the field then describes the spread of the strain field. It does not describe the uncertainty of independent measurements. Every spread quoted on this page will name its step.
